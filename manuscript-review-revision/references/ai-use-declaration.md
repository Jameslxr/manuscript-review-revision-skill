# AI-use declaration

Load this reference when an AI-use statement is added, revised, checked, or
adapted for a target journal. This is content work and requires the authorized
revision lane; a format-only pass may style an existing declaration but must
not add or rewrite one.

## Source and truth precedence

1. Use exact editor-supplied wording when provided.
2. Use the current exact journal/publisher wording when it is mandatory for the
   article type and submission stage.
3. Otherwise use a concise factual statement that names the actual tool and
   actual scope of use.

Never invent the tool, version, use case, review process, or author oversight.
Do not use a language-only template if AI also performed literature retrieval,
evidence synthesis, analysis, interpretation, figure generation, or substantive
drafting; disclose the actual authorized scope instead. Keep the AI-use
statement separate from CRediT/Author Contributions and do not imply that an AI
system is an author or accepted responsibility.

## Journal-neutral language-only template

When the tool was ChatGPT and its use was limited to language clarity and
readability, the personal fallback is:

> During preparation of this manuscript, the authors used ChatGPT (OpenAI)
> solely to improve language clarity and readability. All AI-assisted output
> was subsequently reviewed and edited by the authors, who take full
> responsibility for the final content of the manuscript.

Paraphrase only when the meaning remains unchanged. Preserve all four
elements: tool identity, limited language/readability purpose, subsequent human
review/editing, and full author responsibility. Check spacing and grammar in
the final text; do not reproduce fused words such as `thereadability`,
`authorsreviewed`, or `thecontent`.
